Stage 1: Lead-In (5-8 Mins)

Icebreaker: Money & Consumer Dilemma Roulette

Shopping Habits Question 1 of 5

"Are you a disciplined saver who tracks every penny, or do you tend to make impulse purchases when you see discounts online?"

B1 Speaking Advice:

• Use financial idioms & frequency: "On the whole, I try to save for a rainy day...", "Every now and then, I splash out on...", "I regret buying things on impulse because..."

Aim for 30–45 seconds of continuous speaking.

Example Model Answer (B1 Target):

"Generally speaking, I consider myself fairly sensible with money. I use a mobile banking app to set monthly spending budgets for groceries and entertainment. However, I have to admit that whenever there are seasonal sales, I sometimes make impulse purchases on clothes or tech gadgets that I don't really need. I'm trying to follow the 48-hour rule before buying anything non-essential."

Stage 2: Lexical Input (10 Mins)

High-Frequency Money & Spending Collocations

Click the audio icon to practice pronunciation. Click any card to show/hide the example sentence in context.

Click card to toggle example
Value Expression

Value for money

Worth the amount paid; providing high quality relative to the price.

"Although the laptop was slightly expensive, its battery life and durability make it great value for money."
Informal Noun

A rip-off

Something that is egregiously overpriced or poor quality for what you paid.

"Charging €8 for a bottle of tap water at the concert was a complete rip-off."
Idiom

Save for a rainy day

To put money aside in an emergency fund for unexpected future hardships.

"I automatically transfer 10% of my monthly salary into an account saved for a rainy day."
Phrasal Verb

Splash out (on something)

To spend an unusually large amount of money on a treat or luxury celebration.

"After passing her final university exams, she decided to splash out on a weekend trip to Rome."
Noun Collocation

Tight budget

Having a very limited sum of money available to spend on essentials.

"While studying abroad, I had to live on a very tight budget and cook all my meals at home."
Banking Collocation

Unauthorized transaction

A debit, payment, or withdrawal made from your bank account without your permission.

"I contacted my credit card company immediately to report an unauthorized transaction of €120."

Stage 3: Functional Strategies (10 Mins)

Consumer & Financial Speaking Toolkit

Everyday phrases for negotiating prices, disputing unexpected billing charges, and talking about saving goals.

Querying unexpected fee

"I noticed an unfamiliar fee on my statement that I don't recognize."

Requesting charge reversal

"Could you please investigate this and reverse the charge to my account?"

Cancelling recurring subscription

"I explicitly cancelled this subscription last month, yet I was billed again."

Card freeze security

"I would like to temporarily block my card as a precaution."

Opening a polite haggle

"Is that your best price, or is there any room for negotiation?"

Offering immediate cash deal

"Would you consider taking €40 if I pay in cash right now?"

Bundle discount

"If I take both of these items, could you do a discount on the pair?"

Polite walk-away phrase

"That's a bit beyond my budget, but thank you for your time."

Tracking expenses

"Tracking daily expenses helps reveal where small leaks occur."

Delaying gratification

"Waiting forty-eight hours prevents impulse buying on sales."

Emergency savings rule

"Having three months of living expenses saved provides peace of mind."

Cutting recurring costs

"Cancelling unused streaming subscriptions saves hundreds a year."

Cost-per-wear logic

"If you wear a quality coat for five years, it pays for itself."

Unjustified brand markup

"You are mostly paying for the brand logo, not the material."

Steep discount skepticism

"If a deal looks too good to be true, it usually is."

Reasonable price

"All things considered, twenty euros is very reasonable for this."

Stage 4: Grammar for Fluency (10 Mins)

The Passive Voice in Commercial & Banking Contexts

Use the passive voice (be + past participle) when the action is more important than who performed it.

Present & Past Passive (Consumer Complaints)

was charged / has been deducted / is refunded

"My card was charged twice for the same purchase."
"The payment has already been processed."

Polite and formal way to focus on the error rather than accusing the person directly.

Second Conditional (Hypothetical Finances)

If I had / If I won... I would invest / save

"If I had more disposable income, I would invest in index funds."

Expresses imaginary or hypothetical personal financial decisions.

2. "If I ____________ €10,000 in the lottery tomorrow, I ____________ a deposit on an apartment."

Stage 5: Production & Roleplay (15 Mins)

Consumer & Financial Negotiation Simulator

Select a negotiation scenario, review your role objectives, and start the timer for a 3-minute interaction.

Roleplay Timer
03:00
Role A: Bank Customer

The Confused Account Holder

You noticed a recurring charge of €29.99 labelled "CloudStream Pro" on your bank statement for the third consecutive month, even though you cancelled the trial in January.

Your Secret Objectives:

• Explain that you cancelled the service in writing and demand a refund of €89.97.

• Ask the bank to block all future automatic debits from this merchant.

• Maintain a calm, professional tone using: "I would appreciate it if you could reverse these charges."

Role B: Bank Representative

Customer Support Specialist

You handle disputed transactions. Bank policy requires verification that the customer attempted to contact the vendor directly first before initiating a formal chargeback.

Your Secret Objectives:

• Ask for the cancellation email confirmation or reference number.

• Block the merchant immediately from charging the card again.

• Open a dispute claim and explain that refunds usually take 5–7 business days.
