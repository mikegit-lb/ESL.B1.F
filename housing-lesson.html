Unit 9: Housing, Flat-Hunting & Neighborhood Life

Speaking Out Loud: Apartment Viewings, Landlord Requests & House Rules

Acquire practical intermediate fluency to describe living spaces, inspect rental properties, negotiate tenancy agreements, politely report maintenance repairs to landlords, and resolve everyday friction with flatmates.

Lesson Learning Targets (B1)
  • Ask probing questions during rental flat viewings.
  • Master comparative modifiers (*far more spacious*, *nowhere near as noisy*).
  • Use polite indirect requests with landlords & flatmates.
  • Debate urban city living vs. quiet suburban life.

Stage 3: Spoken Interaction Toolkit

Inquiring During Viewings & Reporting Repairs

Essential spoken stems for inspecting a flat, asking critical lease questions, and making polite yet firm repair demands.

Asking During an Apartment Viewing

Inspect the property carefully and clarify costs before signing anything.

"Could you tell me how much the average monthly bills come to in winter?"

Polite indirect question about hidden utility expenses.

"What is the minimum lease term, and how much notice do I need to give?"

Clarifying tenancy duration and break-clause terms.

"Is street parking readily available, or is a resident permit required?"

Practical consideration for car owners.
Reporting Maintenance & Setting House Boundaries

Communicate firmly without being confrontational or aggressive.

"I'm calling to report that the bathroom radiator has stopped working completely."

Standard opening formula for tenant maintenance calls.

"Could you send an engineer round to take a look as soon as possible?"

Requesting a technician or repair person to visit.

"Would you mind doing the dishes right after dinner so the sink stays clear?"

Diplomatic housemate request using 'Would you mind + -ing'.
Stage 4: Structural Accuracy

Target Grammar: Comparative Modifiers & Polite Inquiries

At B1 level, don't just say "it is bigger". Use precision modifiers (*far*, *significantly*, *slightly*, *nowhere near as... as*) to describe housing trade-offs.

Modifiers for Big vs. Small Differences
Big Difference (far / significantly / way):

"The suburban flat is far more spacious than the downtown studio."

("way more spacious" is natural in casual spoken English)

Small Difference (slightly / a bit / a little):

"The ground floor apartment is slightly cheaper, but has less natural light."

Extreme Negative Comparison (nowhere near as... as):

"The second flat is nowhere near as convenient as the first one."

(Meaning: The second flat is dramatically less convenient)

Indirect Questions (Polite B1 Word Order)

Direct questions can sound aggressive when negotiating. Use introductory stems with normal statement word order (no inversion).

Direct: "Where is the nearest bus stop?"

"Could you tell me where the nearest bus stop is?"

Subject + verb remains affirmative.
Direct: "Does the rent include Wi-Fi?"

"I was wondering if the rent includes Wi-Fi."

Use 'if' or 'whether' for yes/no inquiries.

Rapid Accuracy Check: Choose the Natural Spoken Structure

B1 Review
1. "Could you tell me how much ________ every month?"
2. "This attic bedroom is ________ more cramped than the master bedroom downstairs."
Stage 5: Spoken Interaction

Conversational Scenarios & Roleplay Tasks

Pair up or practice speaking both roles out loud. Use the integrated prep & speaking timers below.

Scenario A: The Flat Viewing

Inspecting & Asking the Landlord

Context: You are viewing a furnished 1-bedroom flat. You notice damp marks near the window and you want to know about building noise and utility costs before agreeing to put down a deposit.

Must include:

• "Could you tell me if the windows are double-glazed?"

• "I noticed some damp near the sill; will that be repainted?"

• "How much of a security deposit do you require upfront?"

Scenario B: Urgent Maintenance Call

Burst Pipe or Broken Boiler

Context: It is November and your apartment's heating and hot water broke down 24 hours ago. You phoned yesterday, but nobody showed up. You call the rental agent again, remaining polite but firm.

Must include:

• "I called yesterday regarding the boiler..."

• "The temperature has dropped significantly..."

• "Could you give me an exact time when the plumber will arrive?"

Scenario C: The Flatmate Chore Meeting

Agreeing on Cleaning & Noise Rules

Context: You and two flatmates share a communal kitchen and bathroom. One flatmate constantly leaves dirty pans for three days and plays video games late on weekdays. You hold a house meeting.

Must include:

• "I think we need to create a fair weekly cleaning rota..."

• "Would you mind keeping quiet hours after 11 PM?"

• "It's nowhere near fair for one person to do all the scrubbing."

Fluency Pitch: "My Ideal Home & Neighborhood"

Structure Your 90-Second Spoken Pitch

1. Current Living Situation (20s):

Where you live right now, whether it's furnished/unfurnished, and its size.

"Currently, I rent a slightly cramped apartment..."
2. Neighborhood Amenities (25s):

What is within walking distance and what the public transport links are like.

"It is far more convenient because..."
3. The Trade-Offs (25s):

Compare city center living with quieter suburban alternatives.

"While downtown is lively, it is nowhere near as quiet..."
4. Your Ideal Future Home (20s):

What kind of property you hope to rent or buy in the next five years.

"If I had the budget, I would choose..."
