Stage 1: Lead-In (5-8 Mins)

Icebreaker: Health & Well-being Roulette

Click the button to draw a wellness question and practice answering aloud with a partner or solo.

Sleep Habits Question 1 of 5

"How many hours of sleep do you typically get per night, and how does poor sleep affect your mood and concentration the next day?"

B1 Speaking Advice:

• Describe symptoms & consequences: "On average, I manage to get...", "If I sleep less than 7 hours, I feel completely drained...", "I struggle to focus on..."

Aim for 30–45 seconds of continuous speaking.

Example Model Answer (B1 Target):

"On average, I get about six and a half hours of sleep on weekdays. Whenever I get less than that, I immediately notice the consequences the next morning. I feel irritable, I experience mild headaches, and I find it extremely hard to concentrate during long meetings. That's why I've recently started leaving my phone in the living room to avoid late-night scrolling."

Stage 2: Lexical Input (10 Mins)

High-Frequency Health & Wellness Collocations

Noun Collocation

Sedentary lifestyle

A way of living that involves little or no regular physical exercise, often sitting at a desk.

"Doctors warn that leading a sedentary lifestyle increases the risk of cardiovascular disease."
State / Noun

Burnout

A state of emotional, physical, and mental exhaustion caused by prolonged, excessive stress.

"After working 60 hours every week for six months, she suffered from severe burnout."
Noun Phrase

Sleep deprivation

The condition of not having enough sleep, either chronic or acute, impairing cognitive function.

"Chronic sleep deprivation can weaken your immune system and cause memory lapses."
Physical Collocation

Cardiovascular endurance

The ability of the heart and lungs to supply oxygen during sustained physical activity.

"Swimming and brisk cycling are great aerobic exercises for building cardiovascular endurance."
Idiomatic Adjective

Run-down

Tired, exhausted, and in poor physical condition, especially prone to catching minor illnesses.

"I've been feeling quite run-down lately, so I think I might be coming down with a cold."
Phrasal Verb

Take up (a sport/activity)

To begin doing a new hobby, physical exercise, or healthy routine regularly.

"To relieve back stiffness from office work, I decided to take up beginner Pilates."

Stage 3: Functional Strategies (10 Mins)

Health Consultation & Advice Toolkit

Key structures for describing physical symptoms, asking for medical guidance, and proposing lifestyle solutions.

Head & dizziness

"I've been feeling dizzy and light-headed since yesterday morning."

Throat & chest

"I have a painful sore throat and a persistent dry cough."

Joints & muscles

"I think I might have twisted my ankle while jogging in the park."

General fatigue

"I feel completely drained of energy, no matter how long I rest."

Strong warning advice

"You had better see a doctor if the fever doesn't drop today."

Gentle lifestyle recommendation

"You ought to cut down on caffeine, especially after 4:00 PM."

Probing suggestion

"Have you tried drinking hot lemon ginger tea before bedtime?"

Empathetic advice

"If I were in your position, I would take a couple of sick days to recover."

Breathing technique

"Whenever I feel overwhelmed, taking deep breaths helps calm my racing pulse."

Setting mental boundaries

"It is crucial to set clear boundaries between work tasks and leisure."

Active unwinding

"Going for a walk without headphones clears my head better than watching TV."

Acknowledging limits

"It's completely okay to say no to extra requests when you're overwhelmed."

Establishing routine

"I make a point of drinking two glasses of water right after waking up."

Gradual improvement

"Instead of crash diets, making small sustainable changes lasts longer."

Exercise consistency

"Consistency matters far more than intense, exhausting workouts."

Tracking progress

"Using a fitness tracker encourages me to hit 8,000 steps daily."

Stage 4: Grammar for Fluency (10 Mins)

Giving Advice: Should / Ought to / Had Better & Verb Patterns

Master the varying strengths of advice and the difference between stop to do vs stop doing.

Degrees of Advice

should < ought to < had better (warning of consequences!)

"You had better rest your knee, or you won't be able to walk tomorrow."

Remember: had better always takes the bare infinitive (no "to").

Verb Patterns with Health (Stop / Try)

stop + -ing (quit habit) vs stop + to (pause in order to)

"I stopped eating late at night." (Quit the habit entirely)
"While running, I stopped to drink water." (Paused to hydrate)

Interactive Quick Check: Select the grammatically correct B1 choice:

1. "You look pale and feverish. You ____________ go home right now before you faint."

2. "If you suffer from insomnia, you should avoid ____________ on your smartphone in bed."

Stage 5: Production & Roleplay (15 Mins)

Health & Fitness Consultation Simulator

Select a roleplay scenario, study your secret objectives, and conduct a 3-minute health consultation.

Roleplay Timer
03:00
Role A: Stressed Patient

The Overworked Professional

For the past 3 weeks, you have had throbbing tension headaches every afternoon, neck stiffness, and poor sleep. You drink 5 cups of espresso daily to stay awake at your computer desk.

Your Secret Objectives:

• Describe your daily symptoms clearly (headaches, tight neck, waking up at 3 AM).

• Ask if prescription painkillers are necessary or if lifestyle changes will suffice.

• Inquire about simple stretches you can perform during office hours.

Role B: Empathetic Doctor

The General Practitioner (GP)

You examine the patient and suspect caffeine-induced dehydration combined with poor desk posture and tension from chronic screen time.

Your Secret Objectives:

• Ask diagnostic questions about water intake, caffeine, and screen distance.

• Warn them: "You had better cut back to one coffee a day, or the headaches will worsen."

• Prescribe ergonomic adjustments, hydration, and a 20-minute daily evening walk.
