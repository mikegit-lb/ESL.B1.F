Icebreaker: Green Living Dilemma Roulette

Click the button to draw an environmental dilemma card and justify your choices aloud.

Fast Fashion Question 1 of 5

"How often do you buy cheap 'fast fashion' clothes, and do you think consumers should pay more for sustainably made garments?"

B1 Speaking Advice:

• Use cause & consequence phrases: "On the one hand, fast fashion is affordable...", "However, it leads to massive textile waste...", "If we bought fewer, higher-quality clothes..."

Aim for 30–45 seconds of fluent justification.

Example Model Answer (B1 Target):

"To be honest, I used to buy lots of cheap clothes every spring without thinking twice. However, I recently watched a documentary about how fast fashion causes water pollution and textile waste. Since then, I have started buying second-hand clothes at vintage markets. In my view, investing in durable, timeless pieces is much better for both our wallets and the planet."

Noun Collocation

Carbon footprint

The total amount of greenhouse gases produced directly and indirectly by human activities.

"Travelling by electric train rather than flying can significantly reduce your personal carbon footprint."
Noun Phrase

Single-use plastics

Disposable plastic items used only once before being discarded (straws, cups, wrappers).

"Many European supermarkets have banned single-use plastic bags in favor of reusable cotton totes."
Noun Concept

Zero-waste lifestyle

A philosophy aimed at sending nothing to landfills through reuse, repair, and composting.

"She adopted a zero-waste lifestyle by buying grains and nuts in bulk using her own glass jars."
Noun Collocation

Renewable energy

Energy from natural resources like wind, sun, and water that replenish naturally.

"Investing in renewable energy is crucial for transitioning away from polluting fossil fuels."
Critical Term

Greenwashing

Deceiving consumers into believing that a company's products are environmentally friendly.

"Consumers are becoming much more skeptical of corporate greenwashing on advertising billboards."
Adjective

Biodegradable

Capable of being broken down and decomposed naturally by bacteria or living organisms.

"These coffee cups are made from corn starch and are 100% biodegradable within 90 days."

Stage 3: Functional Strategies (10 Mins)

Environmental Cause & Effect Toolkit

Connectors and phrases for describing causes, recommending solutions, and persuading others politely.

Expressing direct consequence

"Excessive packaging leads directly to massive landfill waste."

Stating the underlying reason

"Because of rising global temperatures, glaciers are melting faster."

Linking action to purpose

"We separate glass and paper so that they can be recycled easily."

Result clause

"Cities expanded bicycle lanes; as a result, traffic congestion dropped."

Encouraging sustainable habits

"We should definitely consider switching to green electricity suppliers."

Practical everyday suggestion

"Why don't we set up a food compost bin in the communal garden?"

Emphasizing necessity

"It is vital that consumers demand ethical supply chains."

Alternative lifestyle suggestion

"Instead of buying new furniture, you could easily upcycle old pieces."

Appealing to urgency

"If we don't act now, future generations will pay the price."

Stressing collective impact

"Even small everyday changes add up when millions take part."

Refuting skepticism

"It might seem inconvenient at first, but you quickly adapt."

Moral appeal

"We have a shared moral responsibility to preserve our oceans."

Cost vs impact trade-off

"While organic produce is slightly pricier, it protects soil health."

Evaluating real benefits

"Is electric transport truly green if the grid runs on coal?"

Practical compromise

"We don't need a few people doing zero-waste perfectly; we need billions doing it imperfectly."

Balancing convenience & ecology

"There must be a realistic balance between comfort and sustainability."

Stage 4: Grammar for Fluency (10 Mins)

Real Environmental Conditionals & "Unless"

Use the First Conditional (future consequence) and Unless (= if not) to talk about climate action.

First Conditional (If + Present, Will + Verb)

Real future possibility & consequence

"If we ban plastic bags, marine life will thrive."

Don't say: "If we will ban..." (common B1 learner error!).

Unless (= If... not)

Essential condition / warning

"Unless governments act quickly, sea levels will rise further."

Meaning: If governments do not act quickly, sea levels will rise.

Interactive Quick Check: Select the natural B1 conditional sentence:

1. "Plastic waste will continue to pollute our rivers ____________ we introduce strict bottle deposit return schemes."

2. "If the city council ____________ more solar panels on public schools, electricity bills ____________ dramatically."

Stage 5: Production & Roleplay (15 Mins)

Environmental Negotiation Simulator

Pick a green dilemma, assign roles, and conduct a realistic 3-minute discussion with the timer.

Roleplay Timer
03:00
Role A: Eco-Conscious Tenant

The Eco-Friendly Roommate

You noticed your flatmate is tossing unwashed plastic tubs, batteries, and leftover food directly into the general rubbish bin. Your building was warned it will be fined €100 for improper waste sorting.

Your Secret Objectives:

• Explain the impending €100 landlord fine calmly without accusing them harshly.

• Propose setting up 3 clearly labelled bins under the sink (glass/metal, plastic/paper, organic).

• Offer to print a visual color-coded guide for the fridge door.

Role B: Overworked Flatmate

The Busy Roommate

You work 50 hours a week and take night classes. You find rinsing every yoghurt pot and separating food containers exhausting, and your apartment's kitchen is very small with little floor space.

Your Secret Objectives:

• Explain how exhausted you are after long shifts and that sorting feels overwhelming.

• Worry that multiple bins will clutter the tiny kitchen and cause bad smells.

• Agree to the plan if bins have tight lids and your roommate helps set them up.
