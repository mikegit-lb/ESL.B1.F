Stage 1: Lead-In (5-8 Mins)

Icebreaker: Food & Dining Roulette

Click the button to draw a food conversation prompt and answer aloud with a partner or solo.

Cooking Anecdote Question 1 of 5

"Have you ever had an absolute disaster in the kitchen while trying to cook a special meal? What happened?"

B1 Speaking Advice:

• Use sensory and narrative words: "I was attempting to make...", "Suddenly the smoke alarm went off...", "It turned out completely burnt/salty..."

Aim for 30–45 seconds of fluent storytelling.

Example Model Answer (B1 Target):

"Oh definitely! Last winter, I invited my friends over for homemade lasagne. While I was making the cheese sauce, the doorbell rang and I got distracted chatting. When I walked back into the kitchen, the sauce had completely stuck to the bottom of the pan and was smelling awful! In the end, we had to laugh, order takeaway pizza, and eat ice cream straight from the tub."

Stage 2: Lexical Input (10 Mins)

High-Frequency Dining & Culinary Collocations

Click the audio icon to practice pronunciation. Click any card to show/hide the example sentence in context.

Click card to toggle example
Sensory Adjective

Savory / Savoury

Belonging to the category that is salty or spicy rather than sweet.

"I usually prefer savory snacks like roasted nuts or cheese over sweet pastries."
Descriptive Collocation

Locally sourced ingredients

Food and produce grown, produced, or farmed in the surrounding regional area.

"This bistro prides itself on using only fresh, locally sourced ingredients from nearby farms."
Idiomatic Adjective

Mouth-watering

Appealing so strongly to taste or smell that it makes you salivate with hunger.

"The bakery window was filled with mouth-watering chocolate cakes and warm fruit tarts."
Prefix Pair

Undercooked / Overcooked

Cooked for too short a time (still raw/hard) or cooked for too long (burnt/dry/tough).

"The chicken was unfortunately undercooked in the center, so we sent it back to the chef."
Social Collocation

Split the bill / Go Dutch

To divide the restaurant check equally or have each person pay for their own items.

"Shall we just split the bill evenly between the four of us to keep it simple?"
Phrasal Verb

Cut down on (something)

To reduce the quantity or frequency of consuming something unhealthy.

"My doctor recommended that I cut down on processed sugar and fizzy drinks."

Stage 3: Functional Strategies (10 Mins)

Restaurant & Culinary Functional Language

Polite structures for ordering food, complaining about errors, specifying allergies, and describing recipes.

Polite order request

"Could I please have the grilled sea bass with roast potatoes?"

Asking for the waiter's opinion

"What do you recommend as today's house specialty?"

Requesting substitution

"Would it be possible to substitute salad instead of chips?"

Asking for the bill

"Could we get the bill whenever you have a moment?"

Cold / undercooked food

"Excuse me, I'm afraid my soup is lukewarm. Could you reheat it?"

Wrong item delivered

"Pardon me, but I believe I ordered the vegetarian risotto, not chicken."

Unreasonable waiting time

"We have been waiting nearly 45 minutes for our main courses."

Bill error

"I think there might be an extra dessert on our bill that we didn't order."

Severe allergy warning

"I have a severe peanut allergy. Does this dish contain any nut traces?"

Vegetarian / Vegan query

"Are there any plant-based or dairy-free options available?"

Gluten intolerance

"Can this pasta be prepared with gluten-free ingredients?"

Checking spice level

"How spicy is the curry? I prefer mild dishes with gentle spices."

National dish description

"It's a traditional beef stew which is slow-cooked for five hours."

Texture & flavor combination

"The pastry is wonderfully crispy on the outside and tender inside."

Recipe step sequence

"First you finely dice the onions, and then you simmer them in olive oil."

Finishing touch

"Finally, garnish with a pinch of fresh parsley before serving."

Stage 4: Grammar for Fluency (10 Mins)

Quantifiers with Food & Defining Relative Clauses

Learn to describe food quantities accurately and use which / that / who to explain unfamiliar dishes.

Quantifiers: Countable vs Uncountable

too much vs too many / plenty of / a pinch of

"There is too much salt in this broth, but there aren't enough vegetables."

Use much/little with uncountable food (soup, butter), and many/few with countable items (mushrooms, chips).

Relative Clauses (which / that / where)

Defining dishes & culinary places

"Paella is a Spanish rice dish which is cooked in a wide shallow pan with saffron."

Enables B1 speakers to effortlessly describe foreign dishes to non-native speakers.

Interactive Quick Check: Select the natural B1 sentence:

1. "You shouldn't drink ____________ fizzy soda because it contains ____________ sugar."

2. "A sommelier is a restaurant specialist ____________ job is to advise guests on wine pairings."

Restaurant & Dining Negotiation Simulator

Role A: Hungry Customer

The Disappointed Diner

You are celebrating your wedding anniversary. You waited 40 minutes for your steak, and when it arrived, it was stone cold and the garlic mash potatoes you ordered were completely missing.

Your Secret Objectives:

• Call the server over politely and explain both problems clearly.

• Ask for fresh hot food and a complimentary round of drinks for the delay.

• Maintain a polite but firm tone: "I appreciate that you are busy, but..."

Role B: Restaurant Server

The Busy Head Waiter

Two kitchen assistants called in sick tonight, so the kitchen is under extreme stress. You want to make the customer happy and resolve the problem without losing your tip.

Your Secret Objectives:

• Apologize sincerely and take the cold food back to the kitchen immediately.

• Offer a free glass of prosecco or dessert on the house while they wait.

• Ensure the head chef personally prepares the replacement immediately.
