Unit 3: Digital Life, Social Media & Screen Time

Speaking Out Loud: Surviving Screens, Online Dilemmas & Debating AI

Gain the spoken fluency to compare digital habits, debate the effects of social media, weigh pros and cons of artificial intelligence, and negotiate household screen time at the B1 intermediate level.

Stage 1: Lead-In (5-8 Mins)

Icebreaker: Digital Life Dilemmas

Click the button to draw a modern technology question and discuss it aloud with a partner or solo.

Screen Habits Question 1 of 5

"Could you survive a full 7-day 'digital detox' with zero smartphone or social media access? What would you find most difficult?"

B1 Speaking Advice:

• Use conditional & habit structures: "If I had to disconnect...", "I am so used to checking...", "I think the hardest part would be..."

Aim for 30–45 seconds of fluent justification.

Example Model Answer (B1 Target):

"To be honest, I think it would be a huge challenge for me at first. I am so used to using GPS maps for travelling and messaging my friends every morning. However, after the first two days, it would probably feel quite refreshing and peaceful. I would definitely read more books and go for long walks outdoors without checking notifications."

Stage 2: Lexical Input (10 Mins)

High-Frequency Digital Collocations

Click the audio icon to practice pronunciation. Click any card to show/hide the example sentence in context.

Modern Verb / Noun

Doomscrolling

Constantly scrolling through negative, depressing news stories or feeds on your phone.

"I used to spend half an hour doomscrolling in bed before sleeping, which gave me insomnia."
Noun Collocation

Screen time limit

A fixed maximum number of hours spent using digital screens each day.

"I set a daily two-hour screen time limit on social media apps to help me focus on studying."
Noun Phrase

Digital detox

A period of time during which a person intentionally refrains from using digital devices.

"Every summer camping weekend, our family does a complete digital detox without any Wi-Fi."
Phrasal Verb

Unplug / Switch off

To disconnect from technology, work emails, and social media to unwind and relax.

"It is healthy to unplug on Sunday evenings so you can recharge for the coming week."
Noun Collocation

Targeted advertising

Online adverts customized to match your search history, preferences, and personal data.

"I talked about running shoes once, and suddenly all my social feeds were flooded with targeted advertising."
Noun Phrase

Online privacy & settings

Controls that determine who can view your profile, posts, location, and photos.

"Schools should teach teenagers how to configure strict privacy settings on their social media accounts."

Stage 3: Debating Toolkit (10 Mins)

Debating Tech & Social Issues Toolkit

Essential functional language to weigh pros and cons, make generalizations, and state strong opinions naturally.

Balancing two perspectives

"While it is undeniably convenient, one major drawback is..."

Comparing benefits vs risks

"The advantages far outweigh the potential disadvantages."

Introducing an unexpected side effect

"On the flip side, people often forget that..."

Concluding a balanced argument

"All things considered, moderation is key."

Broad societal trend

"Generally speaking, teenagers tend to spend more time..."

Describing typical behavior

"In the vast majority of cases, people rely on..."

Softening a statement

"By and large, digital tools have made life easier."

Allowing for exceptions

"With a few notable exceptions, most apps..."

Unwavering conviction

"I am firmly convinced that AI will transform education."

Denying doubt

"There is no doubt in my mind that screen addiction is real."

Refuting an opponent

"I completely reject the idea that social media is harmless."

Calling for action

"It is high time that tech companies protected children."

Past discontinued habit

"Ten years ago, people used to read printed newspapers."

Familiar ongoing habit

"Nowadays, I am used to buying everything online."

Process of adaptation

"It took me a while to get used to typing on a touch screen."

Contrasting past & present

"We used to remember phone numbers; now our phones do it."

Stage 4: Grammar for Fluency (10 Mins)

Mastering "Used To" vs "Be Used To" & Future Probability

Crucial B1 grammar: distinguishing between past discontinued habits and current familiarity.

used to + infinitive

Past Habit (No longer true today)

"I used to play video games every day when I was a teenager."

Refers strictly to a past habit or past state that has ceased.

be / get used to + verb-ing / noun

Familiarity (Accustomed to something)

"I am used to receiving dozens of notifications daily."

It is normal, comfortable, and not strange to you.

The 15-Year-Old Student

Your Secret Objectives:

• Explain why you need your phone until 10:00 PM for school chat.

• Promise to charge it outside your bedroom after 10:00 PM.

• Maintain a respectful, mature tone using: "Could we please find a compromise...?"

Role B: Concerned Parent

The Caring Parent

You noticed your teenager is tired in the mornings and grades have dropped slightly. You read medical articles about how blue light affects sleep, and you want healthy boundaries.

Your Secret Objectives:

• Express concern about poor sleep and morning fatigue.

• Offer a traditional alarm clock so the phone doesn't need to be in the bedroom.

• Agree to 9:30 PM on weekdays and 10:30 PM on weekends if grades improve.
