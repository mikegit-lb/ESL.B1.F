Stage 1: Lead-In (5-8 Mins)

Icebreaker: Career & Workplace Dilemmas

Click the button to draw a workplace dilemma card and discuss it with your partner or practice answering aloud.

Workplace Dilemma Question 1 of 5

"Would you rather work 4 long 10-hour days and have a 3-day weekend every week, or work 5 standard 8-hour days? Why?"

B1 Speaking Advice:

• Use preference structures: "I'd definitely prefer...", "Given the choice, I'd rather...", "On the one hand..., but on the other hand..."

Aim for 30–45 seconds of fluent justification.

Example Model Answer (B1 Target):

"Personally, I would definitely opt for the 4-day working week. Even though 10-hour days can be tiring, having an extra full day off on Friday would dramatically improve my work-life balance. It would give me enough time to rest, pursue hobbies like photography, and spend quality time with my family without feeling rushed."

Stage 2: Lexical Input (10 Mins)

High-Frequency Workplace Collocations

Click the audio button to practice pronunciation. Click any card to show/hide the example sentence in context.

Click card to toggle example
Noun Phrase

Work-life balance

The ideal division of time and energy between professional duties and personal life.

"Working from home two days a week has really helped me maintain a healthy work-life balance."
Verb + Noun

Meet tight deadlines

To finish a project or task before a strict, limited cutoff date or time.

"In our marketing department, we frequently have to meet tight deadlines under pressure."
Idiom

Climb the career ladder

To get promoted and advance into higher, more senior positions in an organization.

"She joined the firm as a junior assistant and quickly climbed the career ladder to senior project manager."
Noun Collocation

Perks and benefits

Extra advantages given to employees in addition to their salary (e.g. private health insurance, gym membership, company car).

"Besides an attractive salary, the company provides great perks including private health coverage."
Phrasal Verb

Call in sick

To telephone or notify your workplace that you cannot work that day due to illness.

"I caught a bad flu on Sunday evening, so I had to call in sick on Monday morning."
Noun Collocation

Heavy workload

An unusually large amount of tasks or responsibilities assigned to an employee.

"Due to an unexpected heavy workload before Christmas, our team had to do overtime."

Stage 4: Grammar for Fluency (10 Mins)

Workplace Modals: Obligation vs Lack of Necessity

B1 learners often confuse "mustn't" (prohibition) with "don't have to" (optional / no necessity).

have to / must

Strong Obligation / Rule

"All employees have to badge in at security."

It is required by law or workplace company rules.

don't have to

Lack of Obligation (Optional)

"On Fridays, we don't have to wear business attire."

You can do it if you want, but it is not necessary!

can't / mustn't

Prohibition (Forbidden)

"You mustn't share confidential client data."

It is strictly against policy and dangerous.

Interactive Quick Check: Select the natural B1 modal:

1. "Since our company introduced a flexible hybrid policy, we ____________ come to the office on Wednesdays, but many people do anyway."

2. "If my employer ____________ me a promotion in another city, I ____________ carefully before accepting."

Stage 5: Production & Roleplay (15 Mins)

Workplace Negotiation Simulator

Select a business scenario, assign roles, and conduct a realistic 3-minute professional conversation.

Roleplay Timer
03:00
Role A: Candidate

The Job Applicant

You are applying for a Team Coordinator position at a dynamic international tech firm. You have 3 years of related experience and want to highlight your problem-solving skills.

Your Secret Objectives:

• Clearly explain a time you managed a tight deadline successfully.

• State your long-term goal of stepping into project leadership.

• Ask two smart questions at the end about company culture and training.

Role B: Hiring Manager

The Department Head

You are hiring for a role with heavy project deadlines. You need someone reliable who communicates clearly, works well with different personalities, and stays calm under pressure.

Your Secret Objectives:

• Ask: "Could you tell me about a time you handled a difficult colleague or client?"

• Ask: "Where do you see yourself in 3 years' time?"

• Test if the candidate can explain their weakness in a constructive way.
